Assignment: Technical Product Owner & Scrum Master

As Product Owner and Scrum Master, I led requirements, backlog and sprint delivery for re-platforming about 20 legacy FORTRAN engineering applications to AWS.

The Business Problem

About 20 FORTRAN engineering applications, some dating to the 1960s, ran on infrastructure that was aging and increasingly costly to support. External licensees received installable executables.

Stakeholders

Business stakeholders, engineering users, external licensees, the solution architect, the software engineers and the cloud engineering team.

My Role

I defined requirements and user stories with business stakeholders and the solution architect, owned and prioritized the backlog, planned the sprints and ran the Scrum ceremonies, and coordinated business validation.

Owned by others: The software engineers and solution architect owned the technical design, including the FORTRAN wrapper. The cloud engineering team provisioned the AWS environment.

Key Decisions and Tradeoffs

Adapting to a conversion constraint

When engineers found that some modules could not be converted directly to C#, they designed a wrapper around the existing FORTRAN code. I replaced the original conversion story with one for the wrapper work, had the team size it, and adjusted the sprint plan.

Business validation before release

I coordinated business-defined test cases and user acceptance testing in staging, so releases reached production only after business users had validated them.

Delivery and Validation

The team worked in Scrum sprints from a backlog I owned and prioritized. When the technical approach changed, I updated the stories and sprint plan with the team. Business users validated releases through test cases and user acceptance testing in staging before production.

Technical context

Angular user interface; C# application logic, with FORTRAN wrapped in C# where direct conversion wasn't possible; NuGet packaging; SQL Server; and AWS compute, managed database and private networking.

Results

Delivery

The team re-platformed about 20 engineering applications to AWS.

Licensees moved from installable executables to hosted access, giving the business more control over its software and intellectual property.

Intended benefit: moving off legacy infrastructure that was increasingly costly to support.

Lesson Learned

Identify the highest-risk legacy modules as early as possible, so conversion limitations surface sooner.
