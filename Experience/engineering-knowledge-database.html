Engineering Knowledge Database Replacement

Assignment: Requirements, platform choice, and phased rollout

I led the replacement of a retiring Lotus Notes knowledge database so engineering kept access to its documentation, building on the existing SharePoint platform and rolling out in phases.

The Business Problem

Lotus Notes was being retired, but engineering still relied on the knowledge and documentation it held. The replacement had to preserve access to years of engineering reference material.

Stakeholders

Department leaders, engineering groups, the smallest department as the pilot group, and individual engineers with specific needs.

My Role

I interviewed department leads to map their needs, chose the platform, planned the phased rollout, led the migration and prepared users for the new process. I also owned the response to user feedback.

Key Decisions and Tradeoffs

Reuse the existing platform

Rather than buy another product, I chose to build the replacement on SharePoint, which the organization already had.

Pilot before expanding

I started with the smallest department so we could learn and adjust before expanding the migration.

Don't invest in a retiring system

When an engineer asked for an enhancement to Lotus Notes, I kept the request in the backlog for the replacement instead of investing further in a system being retired.

Add hands-on support

When early feedback showed that documentation alone wasn't enough, we added a week of lunchtime hands-on workshops.

Delivery and Adoption

The rollout began with a pilot in the smallest department, then expanded the migration. After early user feedback, we added a week of lunchtime hands-on workshops to help people work in the new system.

Results

Delivery

The legacy system was replaced, with the migration completed in phases.

Quality

Engineering kept access to its knowledge and documentation.

Quality

Usability concerns raised by early users were addressed through hands-on workshops.

Lesson Learned

Plan adoption support from the start. Documentation alone isn't enough to help people move to a new way of working.
