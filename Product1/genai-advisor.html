Executive Summary

PortfolioIQ (originally named the AI Portfolio Rationalization Advisor; the app’s own header reads AI Portfolio Advisor) is a deployed Streamlit prototype demonstrating how a Centaur governance model can be applied to one of the most common and costly problems in enterprise IT: deciding which applications to retain, modernize, retire, or replace.

Enterprise organizations waste significant budget on redundant, aging, and poorly governed application portfolios. The decision process is typically fragmented, spreadsheet-driven, and inconsistent across business units. This application structures that decision into a governed, explainable workflow with session-level decision logging and CSV export: a deterministic rules engine scores each application and produces the recommendation, Claude answers questions about the portfolio, and the final decision on a reviewed recommendation rests with a person.

Built using Python, Streamlit, and the Anthropic Claude API, the app is live at ai-app-advisor.streamlit.app and deployed from a GitHub repository on Streamlit Community Cloud.

What This Demonstrates

Most AI prototypes demonstrate a chatbot. This demonstrates AI product thinking at a Senior PM level: governance, explainability, cost management, and human oversight incorporated into the recommendation review workflow.

Governance by design

Three-layer Centaur model with a recommendation engine, APM review, and CIO escalation. Every decision is logged with reviewer name, timestamp, and written rationale in a session-based audit log with CSV export.

Explainability first

Every recommendation shows eight supporting signals, a rules-based confidence score, and a Jagged Frontier position showing how closely a human should validate it. The rules are deterministic: the same inputs always produce the same recommendation.

Cost-aware AI

Claude is used only for conversational analysis on Screen 6, behind a shared passphrase gate and a 20-query per-session limit with a visible quota counter. Recommendations themselves require no API calls.

Integration-ready data layer

ServiceNow Table API connector with automatic CSV fallback, detected at startup, and a data mode indicator so users always know their data source. The public demo runs on sample CSV data.

The Centaur Governance Model

Named after the mythological figure combining human and horse, the Centaur model pairs automated analysis and AI assistance with human judgment, accountability, and contextual reasoning. It is the architectural backbone of the application and the answer to the question every enterprise AI buyer asks: who is accountable when the AI is wrong?

🤖
Layer 1 — Recommendation Engine

Deterministic, rules-based scoring generates a recommendation (Retain / Modernize / Retire / Replace / Evaluate) with a confidence score and a Jagged Frontier position showing how closely a human should validate it. Claude does not set recommendations.

👤
Layer 2 — APM Review

The Application Portfolio Manager reviews the rules-generated recommendations placed in the Validation Queue and can approve, override (with mandatory written rationale), or escalate each one to the CIO.

🏢
Layer 3 — CIO Escalation

High-stakes, high-risk, or ambiguous decisions are escalated to executive review. Every escalation is recorded in the session’s Decision Audit Log with a standard rationale.

Why this matters: Enterprise AI buyers are under growing regulatory and board-level pressure to demonstrate AI accountability and auditability. That accountability has to be designed into the workflow from the start rather than retrofitted, and this prototype shows one way to do it.

The 6 Screens

Each screen serves a distinct role in the governance workflow. Screens 1–5 are fully public. Screen 6 sits behind a shared passphrase gate to control API costs; there are no individual user accounts.

Screen 1
Portfolio Overview
Executive dashboard

At-Risk Apps, Portfolio TCO, Pending Decisions, and Cycle Accuracy KPIs (Cycle Accuracy is calculated from pre-loaded sample decisions). Interactive multi-select filters by Lifecycle, AI Recommendation (the interface’s label for the rules-generated recommendation), and Business Unit. Full application table with recommendations and status for the current rationalization cycle, built from sample data.

Screen 2
AI Analysis & Signals
Full explainability for every recommendation

Select any application to see eight supporting signals: Lifecycle Status, Technical Debt Index, Incident Trend, 12-Month Incident Volume, Critical CVEs, Stability Inverse, Duplicate Functionality, and Replacement Candidate Exists — each scored 0–100 with an impact badge. Not all eight directly determine the recommendation: the decision rules use lifecycle, technical debt, duplicate functionality, and replacement availability (plus business criticality) directly; Critical CVEs, incident trend, outages, and stability contribute indirectly through the technical debt score; and 12-month incident volume is shown for context. The Jagged Frontier position (Inside, Edge, or Outside) tells the APM how closely to validate the recommendation. A plain-English “Why this recommendation?” panel, generated from templates, makes the reasoning accessible to non-technical stakeholders. A recommendation drift badge is simulated in the prototype.

Screen 3
Validation Queue
The APM's primary workspace

Recommendations awaiting review in the current cycle: four in the demo, while the other eleven applications carry pre-loaded sample decisions. Review is available but not enforced; unreviewed recommendations simply remain pending. Three decision actions: Approve, Override (requires mandatory written rationale — a deliberate governance control that forces the APM to articulate their reasoning and creates a record that can inform future rule tuning), or Escalate to CIO.

Screen 4
Decision Audit Log
The accountability mechanism

A record of every governance decision: timestamp, application, recommendation at time of review, decision, reviewer name (entered by the reviewer), role, and written rationale, with counts by decision type and CSV export. Entries are held in the browser session; database persistence is on the roadmap.

Screen 5
Governance Dashboard
Illustrative governance analytics

Shows the metrics a production version would track: TP/FP accuracy by cycle, SQDC impact, recommendation drift, and cycle history. These values are illustrative sample data, not measured results. The open-decision count and the current recommendation distribution across Retain / Modernize / Retire / Replace / Evaluate reflect the live session.

Screen 6
Semantic CMDB Search
Claude-powered conversational analysis — shared passphrase gate

Natural-language questions answered by Claude, with the full 15-application portfolio and governance context supplied in the prompt (context stuffing rather than embeddings or vector search), chosen for transparency at demo scale. This is the only model-generated output in the app, and answers are advisory. 20-query session limit with visible quota counter. A shared passphrase gate, not individual user authentication, controls API costs. Eight suggested prompts guide users to high-value queries: cost of all end-of-life apps, dependency risk of retiring a specific application, comparative modernization priority between two systems.

Technical Architecture

Key design choices, and the conditions under which they would change at scale.

Stack

  • Python + Streamlit — rapid full-stack deployment without a separate frontend
  • Deterministic Python rules (pandas) — scoring, recommendations, confidence, and frontier position
  • Anthropic Claude API (claude-sonnet) — conversational portfolio analysis on Screen 6
  • ServiceNow Table API — connector for CMDB, vulnerability, and incident data; the public demo uses the CSV fallback
  • GitHub + Streamlit Community Cloud — public demo deployment with automatic redeploy on push; no automated test suite yet

Design Choices

  • Context stuffing over vector DB: At 15-application demo scale, stuffing the full portfolio into the Claude context window is faster, more transparent, and easier to debug than a retrieval pipeline. Retrieval would be evaluated only when the portfolio outgrows the prompt budget (see Architecture & Evolution).
  • Direct Claude API over LangChain: LangChain would add abstraction the prototype does not need. Direct API calls keep prompts and responses transparent and controllable.

Data Architecture

  • Five CSV files representing a 15-application enterprise portfolio: application inventory, dependency relationships, security vulnerability records, business capability mappings, 12-month incident summary
  • Auto-detection at startup: live ServiceNow PDI if credentials present, silent CSV fallback if not
  • Data mode indicator in sidebar — users always know their data source

Governance Controls

  • Per-session API query limit (20 queries) with visible countdown
  • Shared passphrase gate on Screen 6 (no individual user accounts)
  • Mandatory written rationale for every override decision
  • Recommendation drift indicator (simulated in the prototype)
  • Audit log entry for every decision with reviewer name, timestamp, and rationale (session-scoped, exportable to CSV)

Prototype scope: The demo uses 15 sample applications. Simulated: the Screen 5 governance metrics and the recommendation drift badge. Not yet built: persistent storage, user authentication, and automated tests.

24-Month Product Roadmap (Proposed)

A proposed 0→500 customer scaling strategy with four phase gates, each to be unlocked only when the previous phase's key question is answered with evidence from paying customers rather than assumptions.

Hypothetical planning assumptions: The customer counts, revenue targets, timelines, and funding or acquisition scenarios in this roadmap are planning assumptions. They are not validated forecasts, commitments, or results.

Phase 1 · Months 1–4
Validate
3 design partners

User auth, multi-user sessions, decision persistence, prompt A/B testing, Postgres for audit log persistence, CSV import for customer portfolio data, rule tests and evaluation baselines. Key question: Will they pay?

Phase 2 · Months 5–10
Launch
10 paying customers

Self-serve onboarding, tiered pricing (Starter / Growth / Enterprise), read-only INC/PRB evidence analysis with validated citations, retrieval and orchestration frameworks evaluated rather than assumed, model routing — cheap models for simple queries, Claude for complex reasoning. ServiceNow partner ecosystem outreach. Key question: Can we retain them?

Phase 3 · Months 11–18
Scale
50 customers

Bounded tool-using agent with human-approved write-back to ServiceNow, if adoption criteria are met; drift detection v2; fine-tuning evaluated only on consented, anonymized data; SOC 2 Type II; SSO/SAML; AI observability and tracing. Key question: Can we grow without me?

Phase 4 · Months 19–24
Expand
200–500 customers

Approval-gated, orchestrated cycle workflows; a proprietary model only if evaluation and customer data agreements support it; adjacent markets (cloud cost governance, vendor rationalization, tech debt management), multi-region deployment, Series A or acquisition. Key question: Platform or exit?

The ServiceNow hypothesis: ServiceNow's APM module is expensive and complex. Their mid-market customers, companies with 50 to 500 applications, are underserved. A lightweight, AI-native alternative that integrates with ServiceNow rather than replacing it is a wedge, not a threat. One possible path, a hypothetical scenario rather than a plan in progress, is partnership with, or acquisition by, ServiceNow or a major SI partner.
